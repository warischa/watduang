CORRECTION MID-SESSION -- the metric definition in the original brief was wrong (coordinator's own
correction, verified against source before acting on it). scripts/play-exit-probe.mjs's own comment
(the gh#190 block, read this session, file untouched) states plainly: every burst contact carries TWO
clocks -- `t` (when the page's listener RAN) and `ti` (the event's own browser-stamped timeStamp,
i.e. when it was DISPATCHED). "handled gap" = consecutive-contact pointerup->pointerdown interval on
the FIRST clock; "input gap" = the same interval on the SECOND. The probe's own comment: a long
main-thread task "queues the arm timer and the next contact behind itself, so a contact dispatched
inside the arm window is handled after it: the handling gap blows past ARM_DELAY_MS while the input
gap stays at the ~80ms the burst was driven at." The classifier (`classifyBurst`, read this session)
gates ONLY on input gaps; a handled gap over ARM_DELAY_MS with input gaps under it is explicitly
documented as "a runner that handled the burst late, not one that sent it late" -- non-diagnostic by
design, not a gate defect.

ANSWER TO THE COORDINATOR'S TWO QUESTIONS:
(1) Input gaps on croc-bite: 82.1-87.4ms in every one of the 6 runs, never over ARM_DELAY_MS
    (runs input>ARM = 0/6). No gate defect was ever recorded on croc-bite by this probe's own
    classifier. inputGapsOverArmDelay stayed 0 every run.
(2) The handled-gap spread is NOT croc-bite-only. one-bomb sits in a distinct middle tier
    (240-788ms handled, crossing ARM_DELAY_MS in 1 of 6 runs) well above the ~80-220ms the other 12
    routes (including cursed-number, the route the probe's own comment names as the site's heaviest
    transition) stay within on every run. Input gaps stay ~80-92ms across ALL 14 routes in ALL 6
    runs with zero crossings -- exactly the shape the probe's own source comment predicts for a
    busy-main-thread-after-transition-tap effect that is expected to vary by route and is explicitly
    treated as non-diagnostic, not a per-route anomaly requiring a cause of its own.

Note: cursed-number itself does not show an elevated handled-gap tier in these 6 runs (84.3-108.7ms,
same band as the 80-220ms baseline) despite the probe's comment naming its transition as the site's
heaviest -- that comment may describe a different code path/condition than what these 6 runs happened
to sample; not investigated further, out of scope for this milestone.

THIS RETRACTS READING (a) IN THE ANSWER SECTION ABOVE (harness/runner-level cause). Dispatch lag --
"the number that named the CI cause" per the probe's own gh#231 comment, read this session -- sits at
0-8ms on croc-bite in all 6 runs (per-route-gaps-full.txt), and input gaps sit at a uniform ~80-92ms
on every one of the 14 routes in every one of the 6 runs. Both together mean the runner delivered
every burst on time, everywhere: nothing here is evidence of a harness/CI-runner-level cause. What
varies by route is how long each route's OWN main thread stays busy after the transition tap, which
is page-side and route-specific -- croc-bite pays by far the largest cost, one-bomb a smaller one, the
other 12 routes near-none.

The two routes' temporal shape also differs, arguing against a single shared mechanism: croc-bite's
five handled gaps in every run are one large block then four nearly-zero (e.g. 1976.1, 0.3, 0.1, 0.1,
0.1) -- one big cost, paid once, at the front. one-bomb's five handled gaps are scattered across the
burst within a run (e.g. 260.5, 0.1, 139.6, 0.6, 163.5) -- several smaller costs paid at different
points. Each route appears to be paying its own, differently-shaped, post-transition main-thread cost
rather than both hitting one shared mechanism.
